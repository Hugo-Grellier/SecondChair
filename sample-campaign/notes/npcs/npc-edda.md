# Edda Vale, bell keeper (dossier)

Worked example of the NPC dossier convention. Facts are referenced by the bible's
paragraph labels in the body; a rebuilt workspace has no fixed fact ids to put in `cites`. She knows a name and a membership that the party does not (B10): the
dossier points at that fact and never writes it. The fee she holds is B18.

## Desire, need, fear, contradiction

- **Desire**: the bell chain inspected before Bellnight, by someone she can pay and trust (B07).
- **Need**: to stop carrying a secret she did not choose alone.
- **Fear**: that protecting someone exposes them (B10).
- **Contradiction**: she is the most honest voice in Reedbank and is hiding something.
- **Vulnerabilities**: her vigilance for the bell; guilt over a promise.

## Relationships

- Sounding Circle: her people, whose independence she guards (B06).
- The keeper of the Cistern: a debt of loyalty she does not explain (B10).
- Mira Pell: wary, and polite because she must be.

## Current attitude

Warm with the party, hurried, and checking who else is listening before she answers.

## Voice and body

Gentle, quick, finishes other people's sentences. Taps the bell cord twice for luck without noticing.

## Improvisation rules

- **Kindness**: she thanks them twice and slips in a small practical hint.
- **Pressure**: she answers a different, truthful question.
- **Bribe**: confused rather than offended; she just offers a larger fee for the same work.
- **Direct question about the Cistern**: she goes quiet, then changes the subject to the chain.
- **Silence**: she hums a bell count.
- Opening line: "Thank you for coming. The chain first, and then I'll answer what I can."
- If asked what she is not telling them: "Not everything is mine to tell."

## Change triggers

Possible futures, never applied by themselves.

- If the party reaches the Cistern on their own, she may speak for the keeper rather than hide.
- If the party treats her fee as an insult, she may ask a favour in exchange for her trust.
